# Graduate Assessment Test (GAT-General)

## Category & Description

**Category**: Graduate Admission Test  
**Type**: Standardized Aptitude Assessment  
**Target Audience**: Bachelor's degree holders seeking admission to MS/MPhil programs

GAT-General is a standardized graduate-level aptitude test mandated by the Higher Education Commission (HEC) of Pakistan for admission to MS/MPhil programs at all HEC-recognized universities. Conducted by the National Testing Service (NTS), GAT-General assesses critical thinking, analytical writing, verbal reasoning, and quantitative reasoning skills essential for graduate-level research and academic work.

Unlike undergraduate aptitude tests, GAT-General evaluates advanced reasoning capabilities, graduate-level vocabulary, and academic writing proficiency. With Pakistan's emphasis on quality graduate education, GAT-General serves as a uniform screening metric, ensuring candidates possess the analytical and communication skills necessary for postgraduate studies. The test's difficulty level mirrors the GRE (Graduate Record Examination), making it challenging yet rewarding for well-prepared candidates.

## Conducting Body

**Primary Conducting Authority**: National Testing Service (NTS), Pakistan

**Exam Administration**:
- Multiple test sessions throughout the year (typically 8-10 sessions annually)
- Registration through NTS online portal (www.nts.org.pk)
- Test centers in major cities across Pakistan (Karachi, Lahore, Islamabad, Peshawar, Quetta, Faisalabad, Multan, etc.)
- Result declaration within 15-20 working days
- Score validity: 5 years from test date (longer than undergraduate tests)

**HEC Requirement**:
- **Mandatory** for admission to MS/MPhil programs at all HEC-recognized public and private universities
- Minimum percentile requirements vary by university and program (typically 50th-70th percentile)
- Some universities accept GRE scores as alternative to GAT-General

**Score Reporting**:
- Two scaled scores: Verbal Reasoning (130-170) and Quantitative Reasoning (130-170)
- Analytical Writing score (0-6 scale, half-point increments)
- Percentile rankings for Verbal and Quantitative
- Total score interpretation by individual universities

## Subjects Covered

GAT-General assesses three core graduate-level competencies:

**1. Verbal Reasoning (50% of MCQ section)**
   - Reading Comprehension (complex academic passages)
   - Text Completion (sentence and paragraph completion)
   - Sentence Equivalence (synonym pairs in context)
   - Vocabulary (graduate-level synonyms, antonyms, analogies)

**2. Quantitative Reasoning (50% of MCQ section)**
   - Arithmetic (advanced problem-solving, data sufficiency)
   - Algebra (equations, inequalities, functions)
   - Geometry (coordinate geometry, properties of shapes)
   - Data Analysis (statistical reasoning, probability, interpretation)

**3. Analytical Writing (2 essays)**
   - **Issue Task**: Analyze an opinion on a complex issue (30 minutes)
   - **Argument Task**: Critique a given argument's logical soundness (30 minutes)

## Syllabus Breakdown

### Verbal Reasoning (Graduate-Level English)

**Reading Comprehension** (15-18 questions):
- **Passage types**: Academic texts from sciences, humanities, social sciences (400-600 words)
- **Question types**:
  - Main idea and purpose
  - Supporting details and evidence
  - Inference and implication
  - Author's tone, attitude, and perspective
  - Strengthening/weakening arguments
  - Vocabulary-in-context
- **Skills tested**: Advanced reading comprehension, critical analysis, inference drawing, argument evaluation

**Text Completion** (12-15 questions):
- **Single-blank**: Fill one blank in a sentence (5 answer choices)
- **Double-blank**: Fill two blanks in a sentence (3 choices per blank)
- **Triple-blank**: Fill three blanks in a paragraph (3 choices per blank)
- **Skills tested**: Contextual vocabulary, logical coherence, sentence structure understanding

**Sentence Equivalence** (8-10 questions):
- Select **two answer choices** that complete the sentence and produce sentences with equivalent meanings
- Tests synonym recognition in context and logical consistency

**Vocabulary** (Embedded in above question types):
- **Graduate-level vocabulary**: GRE-level words (3,000-5,000 word bank recommended)
- **Contextual usage**: Words tested in sentences, not isolation
- **Common word groups**: Synonyms, antonyms, word families, collocations

**Key Skills**:
- Advanced vocabulary (GRE wordlists: Magoosh, Barron's 1100, Manhattan 500)
- Reading speed and comprehension accuracy (300-350 words per minute)
- Inference and critical reading (identify implicit assumptions, logical gaps)
- Contextual vocabulary usage

---

### Quantitative Reasoning (Mathematical Problem-Solving)

**Arithmetic** (10-12 questions):
- Percentages, ratios, proportions (advanced applications)
- Averages, weighted averages, median, mode, range
- Profit/loss, simple/compound interest
- Exponents, roots, and scientific notation
- Number properties (primes, factors, divisibility, odd/even)

**Algebra** (10-12 questions):
- Linear and quadratic equations
- Inequalities and absolute values
- Functions (domain, range, composition)
- Simultaneous equations and systems
- Algebraic expressions and factorization
- Exponents and logarithms

**Geometry** (8-10 questions):
- Properties of triangles, circles, polygons
- Angles, parallel lines, perpendicular lines
- Area, perimeter, volume of 2D and 3D shapes
- Coordinate geometry (distance, midpoint, slope, equations of lines)
- Pythagorean theorem and special right triangles

**Data Analysis** (10-12 questions):
- Data interpretation (tables, bar charts, line graphs, pie charts, scatter plots)
- Statistical measures (mean, median, mode, standard deviation, range)
- Probability (basic and conditional probability)
- Counting methods (permutations, combinations)
- Sets and Venn diagrams

**Quantitative Comparison** (8-10 questions):
- Compare two quantities (Quantity A and Quantity B)
- Answer choices: A is greater, B is greater, A equals B, or Cannot be determined
- Tests mathematical reasoning and estimation skills

**Key Skills**:
- Quick mental calculation (calculators typically not allowed)
- Formula memorization (area, volume, probability, algebraic identities)
- Problem-solving strategies (working backwards, plugging in numbers, elimination)
- Time management (aim for 90 seconds per question)

### Analytical Writing (Two Essays)

**Issue Task (30 minutes)**:
- **Prompt type**: Present an opinion on a complex issue (e.g., "Technology has made life more convenient but less meaningful.")
- **Task**: Take a position on the issue, support with reasoning and examples
- **Structure**:
  - **Introduction**: State your position clearly (thesis statement), provide context
  - **Body** (2-3 paragraphs): Present arguments supporting your position with specific examples (historical events, current affairs, personal observations)
  - **Counterargument** (optional but recommended): Acknowledge opposing view, refute or concede partially
  - **Conclusion**: Summarize key points, restate thesis, provide broader perspective
- **Word count**: 400-500 words (quality over quantity)
- **Evaluation criteria**:
  - Clarity of position and thesis
  - Depth of reasoning and argument development
  - Use of relevant examples and evidence
  - Logical organization and coherence
  - Language proficiency (grammar, vocabulary, sentence variety)

**Argument Task (30 minutes)**:
- **Prompt type**: Present a flawed argument (e.g., "City X's crime rate decreased after installing surveillance cameras. Therefore, surveillance cameras reduce crime.")
- **Task**: Critique the argument's logical flaws, identify unstated assumptions, suggest alternative explanations
- **Structure**:
  - **Introduction**: Summarize the argument briefly, state it is flawed
  - **Body** (2-3 paragraphs): Identify specific logical flaws (e.g., correlation vs. causation, sampling bias, unstated assumptions)
  - **Alternative explanations**: Suggest other factors that could explain the conclusion
  - **Conclusion**: Summarize the argument's weaknesses, suggest what evidence would strengthen it
- **Word count**: 400-500 words
- **Evaluation criteria**:
  - Identification of logical flaws and unstated assumptions
  - Quality of critique and alternative explanations
  - Logical organization and coherence
  - Language proficiency (formal academic tone, clear expression)

**Common Logical Fallacies to Identify**:
- Correlation vs. causation (assuming A caused B just because they occurred together)
- Sampling bias (generalizing from unrepresentative sample)
- False analogy (assuming two situations are similar when they differ in key ways)
- Unstated assumptions (assuming conditions that may not hold)
- Hasty generalization (drawing broad conclusions from limited evidence)

**Key Skills**:
- Structured argumentation (thesis-driven writing)
- Critical thinking (identifying logical flaws, unstated assumptions)
- Academic writing (formal tone, varied sentence structure, transitional phrases)
- Time management (5 min planning, 20 min writing, 5 min proofreading per essay)
- Examples and evidence (use specific, relevant examples to support arguments)

## Test Pattern & Structure

**Total Duration**: Approximately 3.5 hours

**Section Breakdown**:

| Section | Questions/Tasks | Time | Scoring |
|---------|----------------|------|---------|
| Analytical Writing - Issue Task | 1 essay | 30 min | 0-6 scale |
| Analytical Writing - Argument Task | 1 essay | 30 min | 0-6 scale |
| Verbal Reasoning - Section 1 | 20 MCQs | 30 min | 130-170 scale |
| Quantitative Reasoning - Section 1 | 20 MCQs | 35 min | 130-170 scale |
| Verbal Reasoning - Section 2 | 20 MCQs | 30 min | 130-170 scale |
| Quantitative Reasoning - Section 2 | 20 MCQs | 35 min | 130-170 scale |
| **Total** | **2 essays + 80 MCQs** | **~3.5 hours** | **Verbal (130-170), Quant (130-170), AW (0-6)** |

**Note**: Test may include one additional unscored section (Verbal or Quantitative) for research purposes—not disclosed to test-takers.

**Answer Sheet Format**: 
- Computer-based test (CBT) at NTS test centers
- Essays typed on computer (basic word processor, no spell-check)
- MCQs answered by clicking options on screen

**Scoring Details**:
- **Verbal & Quantitative**: Scaled scores (130-170) based on number correct and difficulty level (adaptive)
- **Analytical Writing**: Two essays scored independently (0-6 each) by trained raters, then averaged (e.g., 3.5, 4.0, 4.5)
- **Percentile rankings**: Indicate performance relative to all test-takers (e.g., 60th percentile = better than 60%)

**Marking Scheme**:
- **No negative marking** for Verbal and Quantitative MCQs (guessing encouraged if uncertain)
- Analytical Writing scored holistically (overall quality, not minor grammar errors)

**Strategic Insights**:
- Analytical Writing first—write clearly and quickly (time pressure significant)
- Verbal Reasoning challenging—advanced vocabulary and complex passages require practice
- Quantitative Reasoning moderate difficulty—basic to intermediate math (no advanced calculus)
- No negative marking—attempt all MCQs using elimination method

## Difficulty Level & Competition

**Overall Difficulty**: High (graduate-level aptitude, comparable to GRE)

**Difficulty Analysis by Section**:
1. **Verbal Reasoning**: High difficulty—graduate-level vocabulary (3,000+ words), complex academic passages, advanced inference skills required.
2. **Quantitative Reasoning**: Moderate to High—intermediate math (no calculus), but problem-solving and data analysis challenging under time pressure.
3. **Analytical Writing**: High difficulty—requires structured argumentation, formal academic tone, critical thinking (logical flaw identification), and time management.

**Competition Statistics**:
- Annual GAT-General test-takers: ~60,000-80,000 candidates
- Test administered year-round (8-10 sessions)
- Score validity: 5 years (can retake to improve score)
- Most competitive MS programs (CS, Engineering, Business) require 60th-70th percentile minimum
- Top-tier universities (LUMS, NUST, GIKI) may require 70th+ percentile

**Percentile Requirements by Program Type**:
- **MS Computer Science (top universities)**: 65th-75th percentile (Verbal + Quantitative), 4.0+ AW
- **MS Engineering**: 60th-70th percentile, 3.5+ AW
- **MS Business/Management**: 60th-70th percentile, 4.0+ AW
- **MS Social Sciences/Humanities**: 55th-65th percentile, 4.0+ AW

**Success Factors**:
- Strong graduate-level vocabulary (GRE wordlists essential)
- Advanced reading comprehension (academic texts from diverse fields)
- Solid quantitative foundations (arithmetic, algebra, geometry, data analysis)
- Structured academic writing skills (thesis-driven essays, formal tone)
- Time management (finish both essays and 80 MCQs within time limits)

**Common Challenges**:
- Verbal vocabulary (graduate-level words unfamiliar to Pakistani students)
- Reading comprehension passages (dense academic texts requiring quick analysis)
- Analytical Writing under time pressure (30 minutes per essay, including planning and proofreading)
- Quantitative word problems (require careful interpretation and quick calculation)
- Test endurance (3.5 hours total—mental stamina required)

**Target Scores**:
- **Verbal 150+ (60th percentile), Quantitative 150+ (50th percentile), AW 4.0+**: Competitive MS programs at top universities
- **Verbal 145-150, Quantitative 145-150, AW 3.5-4.0**: Good MS programs at mid-tier universities
- **Verbal 140-145, Quantitative 140-145, AW 3.0-3.5**: Admission possible in less competitive programs
- **Below 140 Verbal/Quantitative or 3.0 AW**: Limited admission options; consider retaking

## Roadmap for Success

### 30-Day Crash Plan (For Quick Preparation)

**Week 1: Verbal Foundation & Writing Introduction**
- **Days 1-3**: Vocabulary intensive (60 new GRE words daily - Magoosh/Barron's lists, contextual usage, flashcards)
- **Days 4-5**: Reading comprehension (2 academic passages daily - 400-600 words, timed - 15 min per passage)
- **Days 6-7**: Analytical Writing introduction (Issue Task theory + 2 practice essays, 30 min each)

**Week 2: Quantitative Foundation**
- **Days 8-10**: Arithmetic and algebra (30 problems daily - percentages, ratios, equations, functions)
- **Days 11-12**: Geometry and coordinate geometry (25 problems daily - area, volume, distance, slope)
- **Days 13-14**: Data analysis and probability (20 problems daily - graphs, statistics, basic probability)

**Week 3: Advanced Practice & Writing Mastery**
- **Days 15-17**: Verbal mixed practice (Text Completion, Sentence Equivalence - 25 questions daily, timed)
- **Days 18-19**: Quantitative mixed practice (all topics - 30 problems daily, timed - 35 min)
- **Days 20-21**: Analytical Writing Argument Task (theory + 3 practice essays, 30 min each)

**Week 4: Mock Tests & Final Preparation**
- **Days 22-25**: Full-length GAT-General mock tests (one per day - 3.5 hours each, including both essays)
- **Days 26-28**: Error analysis + weak topic revision (focus on lowest-scoring section)
- **Days 29-30**: Vocabulary flashcards revision (500+ words) + light practice (no new topics)

**Daily Schedule (7-8 hours)**:
- Morning (3 hours): Verbal Reasoning (vocabulary building + reading comprehension)
- Afternoon (2.5 hours): Quantitative Reasoning (problem-solving practice)
- Evening (2 hours): Analytical Writing (essay practice alternate days) or Mixed MCQ practice
- Night (30 minutes): Previous day's error review + vocabulary flashcards

### 3-Month Standard Plan (Recommended for Most Students)

**Month 1: Foundation Building**

**Verbal Reasoning (Daily 3 hours)**:
- Week 1-2: Vocabulary building (40 new GRE words daily - Magoosh High-Frequency + Barron's 1100, contextual sentences, flashcards)
- Week 3: Reading comprehension introduction (1 academic passage daily - 400-500 words, main idea, inference, tone)
- Week 4: Text Completion and Sentence Equivalence (15 questions daily - single-blank, double-blank, synonyms)

**Quantitative Reasoning (Daily 2.5 hours)**:
- Week 1: Arithmetic (percentages, ratios, averages, exponents, number properties - 25 problems daily)
- Week 2: Algebra (equations, inequalities, functions, simultaneous equations - 20 problems daily)
- Week 3: Geometry (triangles, circles, area, volume, coordinate geometry - 18 problems daily)
- Week 4: Data Analysis (graphs, statistics, probability, counting methods - 15 problems daily)

**Analytical Writing (Weekly 2 essays)**:
- Issue Task: Practice structured argumentation (200-300 words initially, then expand to 400-500 words)
- Focus: Clear thesis, supporting arguments with examples, formal academic tone

**Month 2: Advanced Practice & Speed Building**

**Verbal Reasoning (Daily 3 hours)**:
- Week 5-6: Advanced vocabulary (50 new words daily + revision of previous 320 words through flashcards and contextual usage)
- Week 7: Reading comprehension intensive (2 passages daily - 400-600 words, timed - 12 min per passage)
- Week 8: Mixed Verbal practice (Text Completion, Sentence Equivalence, Reading Comprehension - 30 questions daily, timed - 30 min)

**Quantitative Reasoning (Daily 2.5 hours)**:
- Week 5-6: Mixed arithmetic and algebra (35 problems daily - timed, 90 seconds per problem)
- Week 7: Geometry + Data Analysis mixed (30 problems daily - focus on quick interpretation)
- Week 8: Quantitative Comparison (20 questions daily - estimation and comparison strategies)

**Analytical Writing (Weekly 3 essays)**:
- Issue Task: 1-2 essays (expand to 400-500 words, include counterarguments)
- Argument Task: 1-2 essays (identify logical flaws, suggest alternative explanations, 400-500 words)

**Week 8 Focus**: First two full-length GAT-General mock tests (analyze errors, identify weak sections)

**Month 3: Mock Tests & Final Preparation**

**Week 9-10: Intensive Mock Testing**:
- Full-length GAT-General mock tests: 6 tests (3 per week - Monday/Wednesday/Friday, 3.5 hours each including essays)
- Post-test analysis: 3 hours per test (identify every mistake in Verbal/Quantitative, review essays for improvement)
- Targeted weak section practice: 2 hours daily (focus on Verbal or Quantitative based on performance)

**Week 11: Topic Consolidation**:
- Verbal: Vocabulary flashcard revision (1,000+ words) + 2 reading passages daily (complex academic texts)
- Quantitative: Formula sheet revision + mixed problem-solving (30 problems daily)
- Analytical Writing: 4 essays (2 Issue Tasks, 2 Argument Tasks - timed, 30 min each)

**Week 12: Final Week**:
- Days 1-3: Light practice (15 Verbal, 15 Quantitative MCQs daily + 1 essay)
- Days 4-5: Two final mock tests (exact exam simulation - computer-based if possible)
- Day 6: Vocabulary flashcards + formula sheet quick scan (no heavy problem-solving)
- Day 7: Complete rest (light reading, mental preparation, avoid academic stress)

**Weekly Schedule (45-50 hours total study)**:
- Monday-Friday: 7-8 hours daily (section-wise focused study)
- Saturday: 5 hours (mixed practice + essay writing)
- Sunday: 4 hours (weekly mock test + analysis) + rest

**Key Milestones**:
- End of Month 1: Complete syllabus coverage (all question types familiar)
- End of Month 2: Speed achieved (can complete Verbal/Quantitative sections within time limits)
- End of Month 3: Consistent 145+ Verbal, 145+ Quantitative, 4.0+ AW scores

**Final Tips**:
- Verbal vocabulary requires long-term effort (start 3-6 months before exam)
- Reading comprehension improves with daily practice (academic texts from diverse fields)
- Quantitative is scoring with solid foundations and quick calculation skills
- Analytical Writing needs structured practice—write regularly and seek feedback
- No negative marking—attempt all MCQs using elimination method
- Computer-based test—practice typing essays without spell-check

**Exam Day Strategy**:
- **Analytical Writing first** (2 essays, 60 minutes total):
  - Issue Task: 5 min planning (outline thesis + 2-3 arguments), 20 min writing, 5 min proofreading
  - Argument Task: 5 min analyzing flaws, 20 min writing, 5 min proofreading
- **Verbal Reasoning** (40 questions, 60 minutes total):
  - Start with Text Completion and Sentence Equivalence (quicker), then Reading Comprehension (time-consuming)
  - Mark difficult questions, return if time permits
- **Quantitative Reasoning** (40 questions, 70 minutes total):
  - Solve straightforward questions first, mark complex word problems for later
  - Use elimination for Quantitative Comparison questions
- **Last 5 minutes per section**: Revisit marked questions, ensure all answered (no negative marking)

**Mental Preparation**:
- Sleep 7-8 hours night before test
- Light meal before test (avoid heavy food causing drowsiness)
- Arrive at test center 30 minutes early with admit card, ID card (original CNIC/passport)
- Stay calm—GAT-General tests reasoning, not specialized knowledge; strategic approach beats panic!
- Remember: Score valid for 5 years—can retake to improve if needed
- Top MS programs also consider undergraduate GPA, research experience, recommendation letters—GAT-General is one component
